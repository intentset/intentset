## What this says for the ADR

**What it keeps current.** At Streamlane's pace (300 merges in 5 days of the window) the +lean subject kept a median 94.1% of verifications current from one commit to the next (newest third: median 93.4%, worst decile 16.4%), against 0% for a commit binding. The median hides a heavy tail: a commit that touches a widely used slice or the lockfile moves most subjects at once (the maximum was 298 of 315 verifications). A record does not last long, though: the share still current 74.7% after 1 commit, 31.3% after 5, 12.7% after 10 and 3.2% after 25. So a subject binding makes a per-commit or per-merge unit run useful, and does little for a nightly browser suite at this merge rate, since a night is dozens of merges and most closures are touched in that time.

**Closure limits.** A verification's subject covers a median 8 slices and 87 files, and the p90 is 26 slices and 200 files (maximum 32 and 228); 6 of 48 slices have a closure of at least half the slices in the model. Subject moves concentrate in a few slices (SLICE-WORK-ITEMS alone is involved in 4080 of 13108 moves), and the slice record itself took part in 63.7% of them, which suggests deciding what of the slice record belongs in the subject (its claims, `implements` and `dependsOn` are what matter to ownership and dependency; its prose is not). This run did not separate edits to those fields from edits to prose, so that is a question for the next step, not a finding. A hard limit on closure size is not supported by this run (the wide closures are the app's central slices, and a cap would refuse to bind them); a warning on closures above the p90 is, since it names the slices that make the gate noisy.

**`exercises`.** Of the 21 end-to-end verifications whose behavior a code fix changed in the same commit, 20 moved through code without any `exercises`, because a slice in its closure changed. The exception (#650, TEST-DELIVERY-STRIP) changed a backend file that no slice claims and a slice outside the verification's closure: it would be seen only if `exercises` named that slice, and with the used resources hashed (+res) it still would not be, because its slices do not list the resource. Of the 176 end-to-end verifications that were only touched by a test file edit, 62 had code changed in a slice outside their closure. So `exercises` is needed where an end-to-end test reaches a backend or a slice it does not import, not on every end-to-end verification; and the larger gap is not `exercises` at all: 129 of 172 steps that changed code changed a file no slice claims (registry resources such as the work backend, other backend files, shared, composition and unowned files), which no subject sees. `exercises` as a list of slice IDs cannot name those files; the ADR should let it name a registry resource, or have a subject include the resources its slices list in `usesResources` (+res), whose cost in this run was a median of 87.6% kept current per commit against 94.1% without. The test's own imports are not covered either: 15 steps changed an end-to-end helper or fixture that no verification's test file hash includes.

**Global inputs.** Candidates that no slice claims and that changed in the window: `pnpm-lock.yaml` (5 commits); the root `package.json` and the workflows changed in 8 commits or fewer each and moved every subject at those commits, for a median 92.3% kept per commit against 94.1% for the lean set. The lean set (lockfile, workspace file, TypeScript configurations, toolchain version files) is the proposal; a workflow or the root manifest is better named per repository, and a changed global input was the only cause of 1138 subject moves of 13108. `CLAUDE.md`, the decisions and requirements documents and the generated help publication change often and affect no check, so they should stay out.

**Where the design is weakest.** Claims decide freshness now, and 75.0% of the code-changing steps touched code no slice claims, so a green subject says less than the ADR's "unchanged since it passed" wherever a backend resource, shared code or composition code is involved. The ADR's own control (the architecture check's unclaimed-file report) would have to be a gate before the binding is trusted, and the report should name the unclaimed files a verification's slices use.

### The decision rule

The rule, set before the run: `entrypoint` becomes the default only if it keeps at least 60% of the store's browser records current at the later commit AND its replayed fixes show no more than 2 false greens; otherwise it ships as an opt-in and the default stays `files`.

Measured: 5 of 237 records current under `entrypoint` at ea7d2808d (2.1%; 5 under `files`), so the 60% bar is not met; 0 false greens among the replayed fixes, so the false-green limit is met. **The false greens are within the limit but the gain is under the bar: the default stays `files` and `entrypoint` is opt-in.**

### What this says

**The rule helps the history and not the store.** Over the 299 steps `entrypoint` keeps a median 98.0% of verifications current per commit against 97.2% (the worst decile 6.5% against 4.5%; in the newest third 47.3% against 21.8%), and the share of records still current after 1, 5 and 10 commits rises from 78.7%, 37.6% and 16.0% to 85.4%, 50.1% and 25.2%. A verification hashes a median 17 files instead of 87 (p90 45 instead of 200). On Streamlane's 237 browser records at origin/main it changes nothing: 10 of 237 are current under either rule at each of the first ten later commits and 5 of 237 at each of the last five. The 60% bar is not met.

**Why not.** The parts that moved under `entrypoint` are not dependency internals. The slices that moved the most under `files` and are only dependencies of most verifications, `SLICE-PLATFORM-PEOPLE` and `SLICE-WORK-ITEMS`, drop out of the twelve most-moved parts under `entrypoint`; `SLICE-PLATFORM-ORGANIZATION` stays at 227 of 237, in its record and in its files, because what changed in it is its public surface: reading the history between the records' commit and the newest (git, not the script), the slice's entrypoint `session-provider.tsx` changed in four commits of the public teams and organizations work (#951, #956, #959, #963, about 200 changed lines) and the slice record's `implements` gained five behaviors (revision 13 to 16), which is a binding field. `SLICE-WORK-COMMENTS` is the same (its entrypoint `CommentsSection.tsx`, and its record). A rule that counts a dependency's contract cannot hide a contract that really changed, and over this window the central slices' contracts did. Of the records current under `entrypoint`, none would be stale under `files`: the store's loss is all in entrypoints, records and the implementing slices' own files, which the rule leaves whole by design.

**The false-green risk is real and did not bite the replayed fixes.** Over the whole window a file a dependency claims outside its entrypoint changed in 95 of 299 steps and at 43 of them `entrypoint` kept current verifications that `files` would have invalidated, 3464 (verification, step) pairs and 269 distinct verifications; 19 of the 57 replayed fix commits moved some verification under `files` that stayed current under `entrypoint`. None of those was a verification of the behavior the fix repaired: of the 27 strong pairs, 26 move through code under both rules and the one that moves under neither, #650 `TEST-DELIVERY-STRIP`, is the case `exercises` was added for. So a verification that checks a behavior is kept honest by its own implementing slice, and the exposure is the one the ADR states: a check that drives a dependency's internals without exercising it. EVID007 reports it, with the slice, the files and the verifications.

**The decision.** The decision rule applied to these figures (above): the gain on the store is 5 of 237 against a bar of 60%, so the bar is not met, and the false greens (0 among the replayed fixes) are within the limit of 2. The first condition fails, so **the default stays `files` and `entrypoint` ships as an opt-in** (`evidence.dependencies: entrypoint`), with its diagnostic and conformance cases. The store's staleness is a property of how often the central slices' public surfaces change in this window; a different cure (`intentset evidence stale` choosing what to run, ADR 0013 "Slow suites") is the one that reaches it.
